Green-Fruit Thinning — UR5 Arm on a Warthog UGV

Research internship with Prof. Manoj Karkee, Cornell University · May'26 – Jul'26

Internship project

A ROS-based pipeline that integrates a 6-DoF UR5 manipulator with a Clearpath Warthog ground vehicle to reach and thin green fruit in orchard trees, from detection to a 3D target to arm motion.

  • Developed a ROS-based robotic pipeline integrating a 6-DoF UR5 manipulator with a Clearpath Warthog unmanned ground vehicle to automate green-fruit thinning: detect a fruit, compute its 3D position in the arm's base frame, solve the joint angles and move the end-effector to it.
  • Built a URDF-based Gazebo simulation of the Warthog (links, joints, friction, meshes) with simulated sensor data, and 3D LiDAR waypoint navigation validated for sim-to-real transfer.
  • Tested a vision-guided path planning system using MoveIt and inverse kinematics, benchmarking OMPL (including RRTConnect), Anytime Path Shortening, STOMP and CHOMP, and upgrading from ROS Humble to Jazzy for newer MoveIt.
  • Planner paths were inconsistent: random paths risked collisions with the Warthog, the end-effector, or tangling the camera cable. STOMP and CHOMP depended on seed paths from those same planners, so the planners were dropped.
  • Deployed a grid-based visual servoing system: the camera view is split into 6 blocks, each with a recorded approach trajectory ending at a pre-approach point at most 0.5 m from the fruit.
  • From the pre-approach point, gain-controlled feedback converts the error between the end-effector and the fruit's 3D point into velocity commands (lower gain within 3 cm). It stops inside a 3 mm cube around the fruit or on timeout, achieving sub-3 mm end-effector accuracy.
  • Resolved outdoor depth failure of the Intel RealSense (IR-ToF) camera in sunlight by fusing calibrated 3D LiDAR and depth camera data: a ray from the camera's 3D point is cast into the LiDAR cloud, the first point of a circular cluster is taken as the fruit, and non-circular clusters (branches) are ignored.
  • Calibrated both the LiDAR (to the arm base link) and the camera so that small measurement errors did not distort the transform, improving 3D fruit localization reliability.
  • Evaluated the detection model with and without fan-generated wind to study fruit occlusion.
  • Started a generic navigation system for ground robots (URDF with sensors, Nav2 and SLAM) for row navigation; not completed within the internship.

Weed Detection & Spraying — GPS-Guided Drone

View on GitHub

Project internship, TechnoS Instruments · Jun'25 – Dec'25

Working prototype

A GPS-guided drone that spots weeds from the air and sprays them directly, with no human in the loop. A YOLOv5 model running on a Raspberry Pi 4 and Pixhawk flight controller turns each detection into a real-world GPS coordinate, then commands the drone toward it and triggers the sprayer on arrival. Currently reaches 0.82 mAP@0.5 on the detection model; autonomous flight-to-target is still being finished.

  • Designed and developed a GPS-guided autonomous drone using a Raspberry Pi 4B, Pixhawk flight controller, Pi Camera, and a sprayer module with an SRD-05VDC-3L-C motor controller for real-time weed detection in grass and precision spraying.
  • Trained YOLOv5-s on a grassland dataset, obtaining high precision and recall for weeds in video, and ran real-time inference (0.82 mAP@0.5 so far).
  • Deployed real-time inference and control through Wi-Fi remote connectivity, MediaMTX RTSP streaming and MAVProxy.
  • Integrated FFmpeg, Flask, PyMAVLink and Pigpio (GPIO pin control) for live video streaming, calculating and sharing GPS coordinates of detected weeds, and fully automated spraying driven by detection results.
  • Everything is controlled remotely over the internet from a laptop; autonomous flight-to-target is still being finished.

Vision-Based Precision Spraying — Jetson Deployment

View on GitHub

With Prof. Piyush Soni, IIT Kharagpur · Mar'26 – Sept'26

In development

A rebuild of the weed-spraying pipeline for an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, running on DeepStream with a TensorRT-optimized YOLOv8 crop detector. Weed regions are found by segmenting green vegetation and excluding the crop area, entirely on the GPU using PyTorch/CUDA, before being converted into real-world coordinates for the sprayer controller.

  • Developed a field-deployable weed detection and localization pipeline for chilli, using green inference to isolate weeds by removing chilli plant regions and extracting weed bounding boxes.
  • Segments green vegetation and excludes the crop area entirely on the GPU with PyTorch/CUDA, using a TensorRT-optimized YOLOv8 crop detector.
  • Converted detections to 3D world coordinates relative to the camera for real-time target localization, and calculated the sprayer rotation angle and nozzle actuation point from weed position.
  • Deployed the control pipeline on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super, using NVIDIA DeepStream for GPU-accelerated inference and reaching up to 30 FPS for weed detection.
  • Established UART communication between the Jetson and an ESP32 to control stepper motors, solenoid valves and the spray pump, sending real-time direction, rotation-angle and actuation signals to orient the nozzle at detected weeds and trigger targeted spraying.

Orchard Classification Using Satellite Imagery

View on GitHub

Remote research with Prof. Manoj Karkee, Cornell University · Jun'25 – Dec'25

Mapping apple orchard canopy systems — V-trellis, vertical, and freestanding — across Washington's Yakima Valley using USDA NAIP aerial imagery. U-Net handles pixel-level segmentation of canopy architecture, while Mask R-CNN separates and classifies individual orchard structures, trained on 1,249 hand-labeled orchard instances.

  • Trained CNN-based models, U-Net and Mask R-CNN, on NAIP satellite imagery from Yakima Valley, Washington, to classify apple orchards into Freestanding, Vertical and V-Trellis structural systems for geospatial analysis and other applications.
  • U-Net performs pixel-level segmentation of canopy architecture; Mask R-CNN separates and classifies individual orchard structures, trained on 1,249 hand-labeled instances.
  • Annotated orchards using QGIS and generated vector masks for precise segmentation.
  • Preprocessed imagery with CLAHE, edge enhancement and georeferencing-preserving downsampling (256×256), keeping inputs uniform and spatial integrity intact.
  • Applied data augmentation to correct class imbalance between freestanding and other orchard types.
  • Trained and fine-tuned both models, achieving about 0.94 overall accuracy, with class-wise instance precision of 0.63 (Freestanding), 0.88 (V-Trellis) and 0.83 (Vertical), and improved minority-class detection through augmentation.

Smart Agriculture Decision Support System

Smart India Hackathon · Sep'25 – Oct'25

Prototype

An AI-based decision-support prototype for farmers that combines hyperspectral and sensor data to analyse crop health, soil and pests, and turns the results into practical recommendations through a simple interface.

  • Developed an AI-based decision-support prototype with a simple user interface for farmers, with GPS-integrated farm coordinate selection through the UI.
  • Used hyperspectral and sensor data for crop health, soil and pest analysis, with prediction and rule-based models.
  • Implemented CNN–LSTM fusion models for anomaly detection and spatiotemporal forecasting.
  • Generated irrigation and health maps from NDVI/NDWI and weather-based ET0 estimates.
  • Built a dashboard for visualization, alerts and practical recommendations for farmers.
  • Compiled data for wheat only for the prototype, from institutions such as ICAR.
